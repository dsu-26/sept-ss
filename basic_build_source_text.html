Visual Hierarchy Visual hierarchy occurs in a composition when some elements “stand out,” or attract attention, more strongly than other elements, suggesting a hierarchy of importance. The term is most commonly used in graphic design when choices are intentionally made to make some elements look more important than others. This order is created by the visual contrast between forms in a field of perception. Objects with highest contrast to their surroundings are recognized first by the human mind. Tools to Indicate Hierarchy Space Size Color Alignment Grouping Graphic signals Capitalization Typeface Font weight Visual Organization Visual hierarchy is an important concept in the field of graphic design, a field that specializes in visual organization. Designers attempt to control visual hierarchy to guide the eye to information in a specific order for a specific purpose. Visual hierarchy is as important in graphic design as grammatical structure is to writing. 5 Common Hierarchy Mistakes Making too many elements compete for attention. When everything is shouting (bold, big, colorful, etc), nothing is heard. Relying only on size. Scale matters, but you can also signal hierarchy with contrast, spacing, alignment, weight, color, and grouping. Careless spacing and grouping. Related items should feel visually connected, while separate ideas need enough distance to read as separate. Not enough contrast between levels. If headings, labels, body text, buttons, and metadata all have similar visual weight, users have to work too hard to scan. Ignoring reading patterns and flow. Important content should appear where the eye naturally moves first, with a clear path through the page instead of scattered focal points. The Squint Test A simple, if unscientific, method to evaluate the visual hierarchy of a composition is the “squint test.” Ironically, squinting or blurring can help us see more clearly. When viewed out of focus (or from a great distance), the viewer is not distracted by details, but can only see overall (gestalt) patterns such as visual hierarchy.
